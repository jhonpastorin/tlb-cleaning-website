# **1\. Brand Foundation**

## **1.1 Brand essence**

TLB Cleaning is not just a cleaning service. It is a team of local mothers from the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast region who bring the same care to your home, rental or holiday property that they bring to their own. TLB turns cleaning from a chore you organise into a relationship you trust, backed by professional systems that make booking effortless.

## **1.2 Company mission**

To deliver reliable, high-quality cleaning across the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast through a team of trusted local mothers, while building a workplace where flexible hours let our people put family first. We make homes healthier, hosting easier and everyday life lighter for the people in our community.

## **1.3 Core values**

•     **Family first.** Our business is built by mothers, for families. Flexible work lets our team be there for school pick-up, and that same family care shows up in every home we clean.

•     **Local and personal.** We live where we work. Clients know their cleaner by name, and our reputation travels by word of mouth through the community we belong to.

•     **Trust and reliability.** Nine full-time staff, established real estate agency contracts and years of repeat clients built without a single advertisement. We show up, every time.

•     **Care in the detail.** From spotless bond cleans to fresh flowers and restocked essentials for Airbnb guests, we notice the small things that make a big difference.

•     **Effortless for you.** Modern booking, automated reminders and recurring schedules mean clients set it up once and never chase us again.

## **1.4 Primary value proposition**

TLB Cleaning gives homeowners, real estate agencies and Airbnb hosts across the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast a cleaning team they can genuinely trust: local mothers who treat every property like their own, supported by simple online booking, recurring schedules and automated updates. By combining the warmth and accountability of a community-based team with the convenience of a professionally run service, TLB removes the stress of organising cleaning, protects the value of your property and gives you back time for the things that matter.

## **1.5 Key brand differentiators vs. competitors**

**Competitive context:** TLB competes with two dominant models in its region. The first is the national franchise (e.g. Jim's Cleaning), where local operators trade under a corporate brand. The second is the national online cleaning platform, typified by Clean4You, which markets across eleven Australian cities including the Southern Gold Coast with heavy trust signalling (police-checked teams, satisfaction and bond-back guarantees, “35,000+ happy clients”), from-price offers, buy-now-pay-later options and 24/7 online booking. Both models are professional and convenient, but neither can show clients the actual local people doing the work. That is the gap TLB owns. Copywriters should treat features like online booking, police checks and no lock-in contracts as table stakes to match, and the local, named, mother-led team as the differentiation to lead with.

### **1\. A local team of mothers, not a faceless brand**

**Problem:** National franchises and online cleaning platforms send rotating, anonymous contractors into people's homes under a corporate banner.

**Impact:** Clients feel uneasy letting strangers into their private spaces, quality varies from visit to visit and there is no personal accountability when something is missed.

**Differentiator:** TLB is a locally owned business led by Teagan, a Northern Rivers mother, with a team made up of local mothers who clients come to know by name and trust in their homes.

**Brand messaging: *“Local mums you know by name, in a home they treat like their own.”***

### **2\. A reputation built entirely on word of mouth**

**Problem:** National platforms lead with volume claims (“35,000+ clients”), discounts and from-pricing to win work at scale, and many cleaning companies rely on heavy advertising to fill their books.

**Impact:** Customers are drawn in by price and big numbers, experience inconsistent service from an interchangeable workforce and churn to the next cheapest option. Scale claims from a national brand mean little to someone deciding who to let into their home.

**Differentiator:** TLB grew to nine full-time staff and secured contracts with major real estate agencies across the Northern Rivers and parts of the Southern Gold Coast without a website or advertising. Every client came from a recommendation.

**Brand messaging: *“The cleaning team your neighbours already recommend.”***

### **3\. A true partner for Airbnb hosts, not just a turnover clean**

**Problem:** Most cleaners for short-stay properties do the basics and leave, forcing hosts to juggle scheduling, restocking and guest-ready presentation themselves.

**Impact:** Hosts stay chained to their calendar, guest reviews suffer over small details and “passive” income becomes a second job.

**Differentiator:** TLB manages backend scheduling directly around bookings, restocks essentials such as toilet paper and olive oil, and adds welcoming touches like fresh flowers, so hosts can genuinely set and forget.

**Brand messaging: *“Set and forget hosting. We handle the turnover, you collect the reviews.”***

### **4\. Proven with the professionals who demand the most**

**Problem:** End-of-lease and agency work has zero tolerance for missed detail, and many cleaners cannot meet the standard consistently.

**Impact:** Failed bond inspections cost tenants money, create rework for property managers and damage agency relationships.

**Differentiator:** TLB holds ongoing contracts with major real estate agencies in the region, a standing endorsement that its bond and end-of-lease cleans pass inspection.

**Brand messaging: *“Trusted by the region's leading real estate agencies.”***

### **5\. Big-brand convenience, small-team care**

**Problem:** Clients currently face a trade-off. National platforms offer 24/7 online booking but anonymous service, while independent local cleaners offer personal care but run on text messages and paper diaries.

**Impact:** Choosing convenience means giving up the personal relationship; choosing the personal relationship means phone tag, missed appointments and constant re-organising.

**Differentiator:** TLB removes the trade-off, pairing its known, personal local team with instant online booking, recurring visit schedules and automated SMS and email updates that match anything the national platforms offer.

**Brand messaging: *“Book in minutes. Set your schedule once. We take it from there.”***

### **6\. Happy team, better cleans**

**Problem:** High staff turnover is endemic in the cleaning industry, driven by rigid hours and low engagement.

**Impact:** Clients get a different cleaner every visit, standards drop and trust never has a chance to build.

**Differentiator:** TLB's flexible, family-first employment model attracts and keeps local mothers who genuinely value the work, producing a stable, motivated team and consistent quality visit after visit.

**Brand messaging: *“A team that loves the work does better work.”***

## **1.6 Target market overview**

•     Busy homeowners and families across the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast, typically aged 30 to 65, who want a trustworthy regular cleaner so they can spend their time on family, work and lifestyle rather than housework.

•     Airbnb and short-stay property owners, both local and remote, who need reliable guest-ready turnovers and want a partner who can manage the process end to end.

•     Real estate agencies and property managers who need dependable bond, end-of-lease and routine cleans that pass inspection and protect their client relationships.

## **1.7 Brand personality**

•     **Warm and motherly.** Caring, genuine and human. TLB speaks like a trusted local, never like a corporation.

•     **Reliable and capable.** Behind the warmth is a professional operation with nine full-time staff and agency-grade standards.

•     **Down to earth.** Plain language, honest promises and a bit of everyday humour. No jargon, no corporate stiffness.

•     **Community proud.** Rooted in the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast, employing locals and giving families flexible work.

•     **Quietly confident.** The work speaks for itself through before-and-after results and word-of-mouth recommendations, not big claims.

# **2\. Messaging Framework**

## **2.1 Brand voice and tone description**

•     **Voice:** a friendly, capable local mum who happens to run an exceptional cleaning business. Warm, plain-spoken and genuine, with quiet professional confidence underneath.

•     **Tone on social media:** personal, playful and story-led. Celebrate the team, share before-and-after transformations and local moments.

•     **Tone on the website and service pages:** warm but clear and outcome-focused. Make it obvious what TLB does, who it is for and how easy it is to book.

•     **Tone for Airbnb and agency communications:** professional and reassuring, emphasising reliability, systems and partnership while keeping the human warmth.

•     **Never:** corporate, stiff, salesy or gimmicky. TLB does not talk like a franchise and does not over-explain what cleaning is. The audience knows what a clean home looks like; TLB's job is to show who is behind it and how easy it is to get.

## **2.2 Key messaging themes to emphasise**

•     **The people behind the clean.** Lead with the team: local mothers, known by name, working flexible hours so they can be there for their own families. This is the brand's core story.

•     **Trust earned, not advertised.** Grown by word of mouth to nine full-time staff and major agency contracts before ever having a website.

•     **Local, not franchise.** A community business that lives where it works, positioned deliberately against faceless national franchises and platforms.

•     **Effortless from booking to done.** Instant online booking, recurring schedules and automated reminders. Set it up once and forget about it.

•     **More than a clean for hosts.** Restocked essentials, welcoming touches and backend scheduling that turns Airbnb hosting into genuinely passive income.

•     **Time back for what matters.** The end benefit is never just a clean house. It is a weekend with the kids, a five-star review or a bond returned in full.

•     **Table-stakes trust markers, stated plainly.** Category leaders make heavy use of trust signals (police-checked, insured, no lock-in contracts, flexible scheduling). Where TLB's equivalents are verified with the business, state them clearly and matter-of-factly. They reassure, but they do not differentiate, so never lead with them.

## **2.3 Language preferences and terminology**

•     Use “team” and first names, never “staff members”, “operatives” or “contractors”.

•     Say “our local mums” or “our team of local mothers” when telling the brand story. Use it with pride, never as a gimmick.

•     Use “regular clean”, “deep clean”, “bond clean” (or “end-of-lease clean” for agency audiences) and “Airbnb turnover”. Use the terms clients actually search for and say.

•     Say “the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast” when describing the service area.

•     Refer to clients' properties as “your home”, “your property” or “your place”, never “the premises” or “the site”.

•     Avoid corporate vocabulary: “solutions”, “premium service delivery”, “industry-leading”. Avoid cleaning jargon and over-technical product talk.

•     Use Australian English throughout (organise, colour, mum).

## **2.4 Preferred calls-to-action**

•     Book your clean online (primary, website)

•     Get an instant quote

•     Set up your regular clean

•     Let us handle your turnovers (Airbnb audience)

•     Talk to Teagan and the team

•     See our before and afters (social, awareness stage)

CTAs should always be benefit-led, low pressure and action-specific. One primary CTA per page or post.

# **3\. Style Standards**

## **3.1 Writing style preferences**

### **Voice and tone**

•     Always use an active voice.

•     Maintain a warm, confident and genuine tone. Capable without being corporate, friendly without being unprofessional.

•     Lead with benefits, not just features (e.g. “come home to a spotless house and a free weekend” rather than “we offer weekly cleaning services”).

•     Use clear, outcome-focused language (e.g. “get your full bond back”, “earn five-star guest reviews”, “never think about the cleaning again”).

•     Use natural contractions (“we're”, “you'll”, “it's”) to keep the voice human and conversational.

•     Avoid cleaning-industry jargon and corporate filler; describe services in the everyday language clients use themselves.

•     Use Australian English and metric units.

### **Sentence structure**

•     Keep sentences concise (15 to 20 words on average).

•     Start every paragraph with the most important, actionable information.

•     Prevent repetition. Cover each point once.

•     Avoid filler phrasing and AI cliches (e.g. “This article will explore…”, “In today's fast-paced world…”).

•     No exclamation marks in body copy and no Oxford commas.

## **3.2 Formatting requirements**

### **Headings**

•     Use sentence case.

•     Follow logical hierarchy (H1 to H2 to H3).

•     Ensure content under each heading immediately satisfies user intent and reinforces trust and expertise.

### **Body copy**

•     Typography, along with all other visual identity elements, will be defined in the upcoming brand design and is not specified here.

•     Use short paragraphs (maximum 3 to 4 sentences).

•     Use lists, tables (with visible borders) and structured formatting for clarity and scannability.

•     Keep bold and italics minimal.

•     Avoid em dashes; use commas, full stops or parentheses.

### **Calls-to-action (CTAs)**

•     Always sit on their own line or block.

•     Keep CTAs clear, benefit-driven and conversion-focused (e.g. “Book your clean online”, “Get an instant quote”).

## **3.3 Link policies and SEO requirements**

•     Link naturally to high-intent, conversion-focused pages (e.g. “Bond cleaning”, “Airbnb turnover service”, “Book online”).

•     Use clear, descriptive anchor text (avoid “click here”).

•     Use trusted, authoritative external sources where references are needed (e.g. Queensland Government, NSW Fair Trading, Airbnb official resources, Safe Work Australia).

•     Cite accurate, quantifiable facts with links to verifiable data sources.

•     Maintain consistent terminology across all content (e.g. use “bond clean” consistently for consumer content, not alternating with “exit clean” or “vacate clean” within the same page).

•     Prioritise local SEO: naturally include service-area terms such as “Northern Rivers”, “Southern Gold Coast” and key towns and suburbs in headings, page titles and body copy where relevant.

## **3.4 Content length guidelines**

•     Cover topics comprehensively yet concisely. Fully satisfy user intent while maintaining authority.

•     Sections: approximately 150 to 250 words.

•     Paragraphs: 3 to 4 sentences.

•     Pillar content: structure for depth, semantic breadth and logical flow (suitable for guides such as “The complete bond cleaning checklist” or “How to make your Airbnb genuinely passive”).

## **3.5 Website and page content conventions (category benchmarks)**

The following structural conventions are drawn from high-performing cleaning websites in the category (benchmark: Clean4You's Southern Gold Coast service pages) and should guide the TLB website build. These are content and structure conventions only; all visual treatment is deferred to the brand design.

•     **Trust signal strip near the top of every page.** Three to five short proof points (e.g. local full-time team, trusted by leading agencies, flexible with no lock-in, and any verified credentials such as police checks or insurance). TLB's version should feature the team, where competitors use generic icons.

•     **One clear primary CTA repeated through the page.** “Book your clean online” or “Get an instant quote”, appearing after the hero, mid-page and at the close.

•     **A simple three-step “how booking works” section.** Check your area, choose your service, pick a date. This reduces friction for clients moving from a text-message habit to online booking.

•     **Location-specific service pages with suburb lists.** Category leaders list serviced suburbs on every location page for local SEO. TLB should do the same for its Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast service areas, naming key towns and suburbs it genuinely services.

•     **An FAQ block on every service page.** Answer the questions clients actually ask: what is included, how much it costs, how long it takes, whether the team brings equipment and products, and how recurring bookings work. Write each answer as a self-contained 40 to 80 word passage.

•     **Client and partner proof.** Competitors display real estate agency logos and review scores. TLB should seek permission to reference its agency relationships and gather Google and Facebook reviews from launch. Only ever use real, permissioned proof.

•     **Guarantees only if formally adopted.** Category leaders promote satisfaction and bond-back guarantees prominently. Do not copy this language unless Teagan formally adopts a guarantee the business can honour; until then use “cleaned to inspection standard” phrasing.

•     **Differentiate within the convention.** Where competitors fill these structures with generic stock content, TLB fills them with named team members, real local photos and genuine local stories. Match the structure, beat the substance.

## **3.6 Generative Engine Optimisation (GEO) guidelines**

To maximise visibility in Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, ChatGPT, Gemini and other LLM-powered search environments.

### **3.6.1 Chunking and passage indexing**

•     Break content into self-contained sections (150 to 200 words each).

•     Each passage should answer one clear question or problem (e.g. “What is included in a bond clean?”, “Who cleans Airbnbs in the Northern Rivers?”).

•     Avoid redundancy across sections.

### **3.6.2 Semantic structuring**

•     Use semantic triples (subject, predicate, object) for clarity (e.g. “TLB Cleaning provides home, bond and Airbnb cleaning across the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast.”).

•     Include definition-style sentences where useful (e.g. “A bond clean is a comprehensive end-of-lease clean designed to meet the standard required for a full bond refund.”).

•     Add descriptors to differentiate entities (e.g. “Northern Rivers cleaning company TLB Cleaning”, not just “the company”).

### **3.6.3 Credible proof points**

•     Use only TLB-verified testimonials, results and client stories.

•     Do not fabricate or import testimonials, case studies or statistics.

•     Use trusted third-party data only for market context (e.g. short-stay market statistics, tenancy regulations).

•     Ensure every proof point is verifiable and sourced from TLB records or a reputable authority.

•     Prioritise real client testimonials and Google or Facebook reviews, before-and-after photos from actual TLB jobs (with client permission), verifiable facts about the business (e.g. nine full-time team members, agency contracts) and authoritative sources such as NSW Fair Trading, the Queensland RTA and Airbnb host resources.

### **3.6.4 Fact and date precision**

•     Use specific facts (e.g. “TLB's team of nine full-time cleaners”) instead of generalisations (“our large team”).

•     Always cite data sources for external statistics.

•     Use full dates or ranges (e.g. “as of July 2026”) instead of vague timeframes (“recently”).

### **3.6.5 Query fan-out and latent intent alignment**

•     Anticipate related client questions, e.g. price and inclusions, bond-back expectations, Airbnb turnover timing, insurance and police checks, recurring schedules and cancellation flexibility. Category-standard examples: “What is included in a bond clean?”, “How long does a deep clean take?”, “Do the cleaners bring their own equipment and products?”, “How much does house cleaning cost on the Southern Gold Coast?”.

•     Address multiple layers of intent (the surface question plus the deeper need for trust, safety and convenience).

•     Consider related, implicit and comparative queries around each topic (e.g. “franchise vs local cleaner”) and ensure relevance to the audience.

### **3.6.6 Source aggregation and trust signals**

•     Ensure each section can stand independently as a reliable, citable source.

•     Write with clarity and factual accuracy to increase inclusion likelihood in AI-generated summaries and knowledge panels.

### **3.6.7 Scannability and retrieval optimisation**

•     Use clear headings, bullet points and tables for easy parsing.

•     Ensure each section is extractable and contextually complete.

•     Use direct, unambiguous language. Avoid layered or rhetorical phrasing.

### **3.6.8 AI readiness principles**

•     Keep content human, authoritative and unambiguous.

•     Ensure facts can be cited without surrounding context.

•     Write in a style that AI engines can easily lift, reference and attribute accurately to TLB Cleaning.

## **4.2 Demographics and psychographics**

•     **Families and homeowners:** aged roughly 30 to 65, homeowners and long-term renters across Northern Rivers towns and Southern Gold Coast suburbs. Dual-income couples, busy parents and downsizing empty nesters. They value family time, community, trust and practicality over prestige. Many were referred by a friend and prefer supporting local businesses over franchises.

•     **Airbnb and short-stay hosts:** aged roughly 35 to 65, ranging from lifestyle hosts renting a granny flat or holiday home to semi-professional investors, including remote owners in Brisbane and Sydney. Financially motivated, ratings-conscious and drawn to anything that reduces admin. They think in terms of occupancy, reviews and return on time.

•     **Real estate agencies and property managers:** professional decision-makers, typically property managers aged 25 to 55 and agency principals. Risk-averse, process-driven and loyal to trades who make them look good. They value responsiveness, capacity and zero surprises above the lowest quote.

## **4.3 Common pain points per segment**

•     **Families and homeowners:** trust and safety with in-home services, inconsistent quality, booking friction, unreliable cleaners and guilt about outsourcing.

•     **Airbnb hosts:** turnover scheduling stress, review risk from missed details, remote management difficulty and hosting workload that never ends.

•     **Agencies:** failed inspections, tight vacancy timelines, unreliable sole operators and reputational exposure from poor trades.

## **4.4 Typical buying behaviours**

•     **Families and homeowners:** discover TLB through word of mouth, local Facebook groups and local search. Often start with a one-off or deep clean as a trial, then convert to fortnightly or weekly recurring cleans. Trust and rapport drive retention far more than price. Decisions are usually made by the primary household organiser, often a busy mum.

•     **Airbnb hosts:** search online, compare a small number of providers and ask in host communities. Evaluate on reliability, calendar integration and reviews rather than price alone. Once a cleaner proves dependable across a few turnovers, hosts rarely switch, making the first month the critical proving period.

•     **Agencies:** procure through relationships and referrals within the industry. Trial a provider on a handful of jobs before adding them to the preferred trades list. Value invoicing simplicity, communication and capacity. A single well-handled emergency clean can win an entire rent roll.

## **4.5 Summary and messaging implications for copywriters**

•     **Speak to pain points in terms of cost and consequence.** For families it is lost weekends and the discomfort of strangers in the home. For hosts it is bad reviews and lost bookings. For agencies it is failed inspections, rework and reputational damage. Always show what inaction costs.

•     **Lead with the team, not the service.** The audience already understands cleaning. What differentiates TLB is who does it: local mothers, known by name, working flexible hours around their own families. Every piece of content should carry some of this human story.

•     **Emphasise reliability, consistency and local support.** Nine full-time staff, agency contracts and a word-of-mouth reputation are the backbone of every trust claim. Repeat them concretely and often.

•     **Use proof, not adjectives.** Real before-and-after photos, genuine reviews, named team members and verifiable facts beat superlatives every time. Never fabricate proof points.

•     **Position TLB as a partner, not a supplier.** Especially for hosts and agencies: TLB manages schedules, restocks essentials and protects reputations. The relationship is ongoing, not transactional.

•     **Address safety and compliance concerns directly.** Reassure clients about vetting, insurance and professionalism in plain language, and only ever state credentials that are verified with the business.

•     **Encourage proactive rather than reactive buying.** Sell the recurring clean, the standing turnover arrangement and the preferred-trade relationship, not just the one-off emergency job.

•     **Leverage the recurring rhythm of cleaning.** Weekly and fortnightly home cleans, every-checkout turnovers and every-tenancy bond cleans create natural subscription-style messaging: set it up once, and it just happens.

•     **Reinforce the local advantage.** TLB lives where it works. Local knowledge, fast response across the Northern Rivers and Southern Gold Coast, and money staying in the community are persistent themes against national franchises and platforms.

# **5\. Content Guidelines**

## **5.1 Quality requirements**

•     Every piece of content must be accurate, benefit-led and easy to read at a glance. Write for a busy parent scrolling on a phone.

•     Reflect the brand voice defined in section 2: warm, plain-spoken, locally grounded and quietly confident. If a sentence could appear on a franchise's brochure, rewrite it.

•     Lead with outcomes (time back, bond returned, five-star reviews) and support with specifics (team size, services, systems).

•     Include the human element wherever natural: names, faces, team stories and local references.

•     All content must satisfy the style standards in section 3, including Australian English, sentence-case headings, active voice, no exclamation marks and no em dashes.

•     Proofread everything. Spelling or grammar errors directly undermine a brand built on care and attention to detail.

## **5.2 Source citation standards**

•     Use only verified TLB facts: team size, service area, agency relationships, services offered and launch details as confirmed by the business.

•     Testimonials and reviews must be real, attributable to actual TLB clients and used with permission. Never fabricate, paraphrase into stronger claims or import reviews from elsewhere.

•     Before-and-after imagery must come from genuine TLB jobs, used with client permission and with no identifying personal details visible.

•     External statistics (e.g. short-stay market data, tenancy trends) must come from reputable sources such as government bodies, the Queensland Residential Tenancies Authority, NSW Fair Trading or recognised industry research, and must be cited with a link and date.

•     When in doubt about any fact or claim, confirm with Teagan or the TLB team before publishing.

## **5.3 Compliance considerations**

•     **Australian Consumer Law:** all claims about services, pricing and outcomes must be truthful and substantiated. Never guarantee outcomes TLB cannot control (e.g. do not promise “100% bond back guaranteed” unless the business has formally adopted and can honour such a guarantee; otherwise use “cleaned to inspection standard” style language).

•     **Honest comparison:** competitor comparisons must be factual and fair. In public-facing content, position against the franchise and national-platform models generally rather than naming competitors. Any reference to a named competitor (e.g. in internal strategy documents) must be limited to publicly verifiable facts and must never be disparaging or misleading.

•     **Privacy:** never publish client names, addresses, property details or images without explicit permission. Take particular care with Airbnb properties, where locations are commercially sensitive.

•     **Team representation:** the team story is central to the brand, but only share team members' names, photos and personal stories with their consent. Represent the flexible, family-first workplace accurately.

•     **Workplace health and safety:** where content touches on cleaning products, methods or safety, align with Safe Work Australia and state WHS guidance and avoid advice that could cause harm if followed incorrectly.

•     **Credentials:** only state insurance, police checks, licences or certifications that are current and verified with the business.

•     **No exaggeration:** avoid superlatives that cannot be evidenced (“the best cleaners on the Southern Gold Coast”). TLB's proof is real growth, real contracts and real reviews. Let those carry the claims.
