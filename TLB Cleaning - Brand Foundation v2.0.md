## **What changed in this version**

Version 1.0 was written before the website copy existed. Writing the pages surfaced several places where the foundation and the live positioning pulled in different directions.

**The mother-led framing is no longer the overarching position.** It is a founder story and it works beautifully in that role, in the about page, the team section and social content. It does not work as the headline positioning, because it describes the workforce rather than what a client gets. Section 3.1 is now local and embedded, professionally run. Section 2.2 carries Teagan's story properly.

**Pricing is no longer framed defensively.** "We are not the cheapest" is replaced with a position built on certainty.

**Geography leads with the whole Northern Rivers**, inland and coastal, rather than the recognisable coastal names.

**The full-time staff claim is removed**, because it is not accurate.

**A rule added on competitor framing.** Prose says what TLB does. Comparisons live in a table.

**New material added:** audience depth, objection handling, a competitor picture, worked examples, and guidance on how TLB should appear in AI-generated answers.

# **1\. Brand Essence**

## **1.1 Core Idea**

TLB Cleaning is a Northern Rivers cleaning company. Local team, run properly. Easy to book, and managed for you.

## **1.2 Positioning Statement**

For households, holiday let owners and property managers across the Northern Rivers, the Tweed and the Southern Gold Coast, TLB Cleaning provides residential and commercial cleaning delivered by a local team on a set schedule, so cleaning stops being something you manage.

## **1.3 The Line**

**Easy to book, instant pricing, cost effective.**

This appears in the subheadline on every service page and as a section heading. It is the shortest complete statement of what TLB offers, and it answers the three questions people actually have: is this going to be a hassle, what will it cost, and am I being overcharged.

## **1.4 Supporting Line**

**Managed for you.** Three words that carry the proposition. Set day, set team, a reminder before, a message on the way, and told first when something changes.

## **1.5 What Business We Are Actually In**

Not cleaning. Nobody wants a clean house as such, they want to stop thinking about the house.

What a client is buying is the removal of a recurring low-level obligation. Not wondering whether somebody is coming this week. Not chasing a reply. Not being the one who has to raise it when the bathroom quietly stops being done properly.

Every piece of copy should be traceable back to that. If a sentence is about cleaning rather than about not having to think about cleaning, it is probably a feature rather than a benefit.

## **2.2 The Founder Story**

TLB was started by **Teagan Burke**, an Alstonville local, who built the business one recommendation at a time across the Northern Rivers. No website, no advertising, just people telling other people about her.

She started it around her own life, as a mother, building work that fitted a family. That is a genuine part of the story and it should be told. It explains why the business is structured the way it is, why the team looks the way it does, and why people stay.

**Where the mother-led story belongs:**

* The about page and the team section  
* Social content, particularly founder-led posts and anything introducing the team  
* Recruitment copy, where it is a real advantage  
* Any long-form piece about how the business started  
* Local press, community content and award submissions

**Where it does not belong:**

* The homepage headline or subheadline  
* Service page positioning  
* Ad copy  
* Trust bars and proof points  
* Anywhere it substitutes for saying what a client actually gets

The distinction is simple. It is a founder story, not the proposition. It explains where TLB came from. It does not explain why somebody should book.

**On naming Teagan.** She is credited as the founder, not fronted as the brand. TLB has to transcend one individual, for the plain reason that a business dependent on one name cannot grow and cannot be sold. Use "TLB" and "our team" in calls to action and service copy, not "Teagan and the team".

## **2.3 Core Values**

**Local and embedded.** Everyone who works for TLB lives here. Not a licence holder trading under a national name, and not contractors driving down from Brisbane for the job. Our teams clean in the same towns they live in and shop in.

**Run properly.** Set days, set teams, and a schedule nobody has to hold in their head. The professionalism shows up in what a client experiences, not in what we say about ourselves.

**Straight with people.** If somebody needs less than they asked for, we say so. If something is not covered, we say that too. If a date is going to move, they hear it from us first.

**Care for the home.** Somebody's house is their space, and we work in it that way.

## **2.4 Verified Facts**

Use only what is confirmed. Where a figure is not confirmed, use a process promise instead rather than deleting the sentence.

| Claim | Status |
| ----- | ----- |
| Local teams right across the Northern Rivers and the Tweed | Confirm the teams are genuinely spread, not one team travelling |
| 98% of our clients stay with us | Confirm the figure and the period it covers |
| Cleaning five-star holiday lets across the region | Confirm, and that hosts are comfortable being referenced |
| Preferred supplier for real estate agencies in the region | Confirm whether these are formal preferred-supplier arrangements or ongoing contracts, and match the wording to the reality |
| Over 100 local homes, hosts and businesses | Confirm the number. Round down, never up |

| Grown by word of mouth, without advertising | Confirmed |
| Full Airbnb management available | Confirm what is included, and whether standard or on request |

**Never claim** that the team is employed full-time. It is not accurate and it has been removed from all page copy.

**Never claim** a bond-back or satisfaction guarantee of any kind.

**Police checked and insured** appears in supplied meta descriptions. Confirm both before publishing.

# **3\. Points of Difference**

## **3.1 Local and Embedded, Professionally Run**

Two halves, and both matter.

Everyone who works for TLB lives in the region. The teams are spread across the Northern Rivers and the Tweed, inland and on the coast, and they clean in the towns they live in.

Behind that is a business that runs properly. Every client has a set day and a set team. They know who is coming and when, they get a message when we are on the way, and if something needs to change they hear it from us first. There is cover when somebody is away, and accountability when something is not right.

Most people assume they have to choose between the two. TLB does not ask them to.

## **3.2 Price and Commitment**

**You know the price before we start, not after.**

Quotes are given on the actual property rather than on a guess. If a client needs less than they asked for, we say so. A one-off deep clean followed by a fortnightly usually works out better than paying for a long first regular clean, and we would rather tell them that than take the bigger booking.

No lock-in, no minimum term, no exit fee. Skip a visit when away, and the spot stays theirs.

**Never use the words cheap, budget, affordable, competitive rates or premium.** Each signals in a direction we do not want. The position is certainty, not a price point.

"Cost effective" is acceptable only when attached to a reason: the same team each visit working to a set routine, so nobody is relearning the house.

## **3.3 Easy to Book**

Online in a couple of minutes, or by text, phone or email, whichever the client would actually do.

A reminder in the lead-up so the day never sneaks up, and a message on the way. For holiday lets, we work straight off the booking calendar so turnovers happen around guests without the owner organising anything.

Then we turn up and get on with it. Some clients want a quick chat and a walk through, others would rather let us in and come home to it done. Both are fine, and they can change their mind whenever they like.

## **3.4 One Provider, Whole Property**

Regular cleaning, deep cleans, end of lease, holiday let turnovers, carpets, windows, gutters, pressure cleaning, ovens and commercial. One provider, one point of contact, one team who already knows the place.

The value is not the list. It is not having to brief four different people about the same house.

# **4\. Services**

## **4.1 Residential**

Regular home cleaning, weekly, fortnightly or monthly. Deep and one-off cleans. End of lease and bond cleans. Cleaning for older clients and people with support needs, including through Home Care Packages and DVA.

## **4.2 Holiday Lets and Short Stay**

Turnovers timed to the booking calendar, restocking, guest-ready presentation. **Full management of the property is available** where an owner would rather hand the whole thing over.

## **4.3 Real Estate and Property Management**

Bond and end of lease cleans, vacancy turnarounds, routine managed-property cleans.

## **4.4 Around the Property**

Carpet steam cleaning, windows, gutters, pressure cleaning, ovens.

## **4.5 Commercial**

Offices, shopfronts and premises, on a schedule that suits how the business trades.

## **4.6 Which Service Is Which**

The three residential services get confused constantly, so the distinction needs to be clear in every piece of copy.

|  | Deep clean | Regular clean | End of lease |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **What it is** | A one-off reset, room by room | Ongoing upkeep on a set schedule | A clean measured against an exit inspection |
| **The standard** | Back to a baseline | Held at that baseline | Whatever the inspection requires |
| **When** | Moving in, before guests, spring, after a long gap | Weekly, fortnightly or monthly | When the keys go back |
| **Who decides it passed** | The client | The client | The agent or landlord |

## **Most people should book a deep clean first and set up a regular clean afterwards. It costs less that way round, because the deep clean does the heavy work once rather than paying for a long first regular visit. Say this. It builds more trust than it costs in revenue.**

# **PART TWO — THE AUDIENCES**

Five audiences. They want different things, worry about different things, and are reached in different ways. Writing to "customers" as one group is the fastest way to write something nobody responds to.

---

# **5\. Households**

## **5.1 Who They Are**

Working households across the region. Often two incomes, often kids, sometimes a business run from home. Time-poor rather than money-poor, but conscious of what things cost.

Also: older clients, people managing a health condition, and households where somebody has support needs. These are not a separate market, they are part of the same one, and they care about the same things with more at stake.

## **5.2 What They Actually Want**

Not a clean house. To stop thinking about the house.

They have usually tried something before. A cleaner who was great and then moved away. A platform where a different person turned up each time. A relative who helps and now it is awkward.

## **5.3 What They Worry About**

**Who is in my house.** This is the biggest one and it is rarely said out loud. Somebody is in their home when they are not there, around their things and their children's things.

**Whether it will last.** They do not want to solve this again in six months.

**Being judged.** Particularly for deep cleans and anyone who has let things go. This is a real emotional barrier and it stops people booking.

**Being locked in.** Subscriptions, minimum terms and exit fees.

**Being upsold.** Booking a clean and being told they need four other things.

## **5.4 What Moves Them**

Meeting the person before the first visit. The same faces. A reminder so it never sneaks up. Knowing the price upfront. Being told they need less than they asked for. Somebody local who they could run into at the shops, which cuts both ways and they know it.

## **5.5 Language That Works**

"Stop thinking about it." "The same faces." "Set it up once." "Nothing you say will surprise us." "We will tell you if you need less."

## **5.6 Language That Fails**

"Sparkling." "Spotless." "Transform your home." "Your time is precious." Anything that sounds like a national franchise. Anything implying their house is currently a problem.

---

# **6\. Holiday Let and Short-Stay Owners**

## **6.1 Who They Are**

Owners of one to a handful of properties across Byron, Ballina, Lennox, the Tweed coast and the Southern Gold Coast. Some live locally, many do not. Some self-manage, some use a manager.

## **6.2 What They Actually Want**

Not to think about turnovers. They are capable of organising a cleaner themselves, which is exactly why the value is in not having to.

## **6.3 What They Worry About**

**A bad review.** One turnover missed or done badly and it is public, permanent and costs future bookings.

**Same-day turnarounds.** Checkout at 10, check-in at 2\. No margin.

**Not being there.** They often cannot verify anything themselves.

**Consumables.** Running out of something small and having a guest mention it.

**Changed bookings.** A cancellation or extension that nobody told the cleaner about.

## **6.4 What Moves Them**

Working straight off the booking calendar rather than being messaged each time. Restocking handled. Photos after a turnover. Somebody who understands that a five-star listing is the product. Full management as an option.

## **6.5 Language That Works**

"We work to your booking calendar, not the other way around." "Guest-ready before check-in." "No messaging a cleaner between guests." "Hand the whole thing over."

## **6.6 Language That Fails**

Anything implying they should be more organised. Anything treating a holiday let like a house.

---

# **7\. Real Estate Agencies and Property Managers**

## **7.1 Who They Are**

Property managers running a rent roll across the region. Under-resourced, managing more properties than they should be, and judged on vacancy days.

They are not the end client. They are a referral channel, and one good relationship is worth dozens of individual bookings.

## **7.2 What They Actually Want**

Not to have to think about whether it got done. A supplier who makes them look organised to their landlords and does not create work.

## **7.3 What They Worry About**

**A bond dispute.** A clean that does not pass creates a tribunal matter, an unhappy tenant and an unhappy landlord.

**Vacancy days.** Every day empty is money lost and a landlord asking why.

**Capacity.** A sole operator who can do one property but not five in a week.

**Being chased.** Having to follow up a supplier is the thing that ends the relationship.

## **7.4 What Moves Them**

Passing the inspection. Turnaround speed. Capacity across a rent roll. Being told proactively when something will not be ready. Invoicing that does not create work.

## **7.5 Language That Works**

"Cleaned to the standard your exit inspection is measured against." "The capacity to handle a rent roll rather than one job at a time." "You will hear it from us first."

## **7.6 Language That Fails**

Consumer language. Anything about making a home lovely. They want compliance and speed.

---

# **8\. Commercial**

## **8.1 Who They Are**

Offices, shopfronts, clinics and small premises across the region. Usually the owner or office manager making the decision.

## **8.2 What They Actually Want**

For the place to be right when it opens, without managing it.

## **8.3 What They Worry About**

**Being seen cleaning.** Anything that interrupts trade or is visible to customers.

**Keys and alarms.** After-hours access to a premises with stock in it.

**Consistency.** Standards slipping after the first month.

**Contracts.** Being locked into something they cannot get out of.

## **8.4 What Moves Them**

Working around trading hours. A set schedule. No lock-in. The same team so keys and alarm codes stay with people who are known.

# **9\. Voice and Style**

## **9.1 Voice**

Warm, plain and direct. Somebody local who knows what they are doing and does not need to oversell it.

Three qualities, all present at once rather than one at a time:

**Straight.** Says the useful thing, including when the useful thing costs us a booking.

**Warm.** Talking to a person about their home, not addressing a market.

**Competent.** Quiet confidence. Never needs an exclamation mark to make a point.

## **9.2 Language Rules**

* Australian English  
* Active voice  
* Sentence case headings  
* No exclamation marks  
* **No em dashes.** Commas, full stops and brackets instead  
* No Oxford commas  
* Contractions encouraged  
* Sentences of 15 to 20 words  
* Paragraphs of three to four sentences  
* Sections of 150 to 250 words

## **9.3 What We Say**

**Our team**, **our local team**, **your team**. Not "our local mums" or "our team of local mothers" in positioning copy. The people who work here are local, and that is the relevant fact for a client.

**TLB** and **our team** in calls to action, not "Teagan and the team".

**Your home**, **your place**, **your property**.

**The Northern Rivers** as the identity. **The Tweed** and **the Southern Gold Coast** as extensions.

## **9.4 The Rule on Competitors**

**Prose says what TLB does and does well. Comparisons live in a comparison table, never in body copy.**

No "nobody turns up". No "you have to choose". No characterising what a national brand or an independent cleaner fails to do, in running text.

A page that spends its time on what other people get wrong sounds defensive, and invites the reader to wonder who we are arguing with. A comparison table is expected, scannable, and reads as information rather than complaint.

Where a table is used, keep the rows neutral and factual. "Whoever is rostered that day" is a fact. "They will let you down" is not.

## **9.5 Calls to Action**

**Get an instant quote** — primary across all pages **Book your clean online** **Set up your regular clean** **Let us handle your turnovers** **Talk to our team** **See our before and afters** **Meet the team**

One primary CTA per page, repeated. Not several competing.

# **10\. The Competitive Picture**

Three types of competitor. Each is beaten differently, and none of them is named in customer-facing copy.

## **10.1 National Brands and Platforms**

Jim's, Absolute Domestics, booking apps and similar.

**Their strength:** easy to book, recognisable, instant availability. **Their weakness:** the cleaner is subcontracted, rarely the same person twice, and accountability sits with a support ticket.

**How TLB wins:** by matching the convenience rather than conceding it. Online booking, reminders, and a set schedule, plus the things a platform cannot do.

**The mistake to avoid:** positioning as the warm local alternative to slick technology. That concedes convenience, which is the thing most clients actually want.

## **10.2 Independent Local Cleaners**

One person, often excellent, usually found through a community group or a recommendation.

**Their strength:** genuinely personal, known, often cheaper, and the client feels good about supporting them. **Their weakness:** no cover when they are sick or away, booking by text, and limited capacity.

**How TLB wins:** on continuity and cover, not on quality. Never suggest an independent cleaner does worse work, because the reader has probably had a good one.

**The mistake to avoid:** any framing that sounds like looking down on a sole operator. In this region that is a real person the reader knows.

## **10.3 Regional Multi-Operator Businesses**

The closest direct competitors, similar size and shape.

**How TLB wins:** proof and specificity. Published task lists, real before and afters, retention numbers, named towns across the whole region, and the same team every visit.

**The mistake to avoid:** generic claims. When two businesses look the same, the one with numbers and published detail wins.

## **10.4 The One Line That Sums It Up**

**Easy like a national brand, local like the person your neighbour recommended.**

Internal framing, not a strapline. It is the test to apply to any piece of copy: does this give away one half to claim the other?

# **11\. Objections**

The eight things that stop somebody booking, and the answer to each. These belong in FAQs, sales conversations and ad copy.

## **11.1 "I don't know who's coming into my house"**

Meet the team looking after your place before the first visit. They live in your area. How we get in is agreed when you book, and you get a message when we are on the way.

## **11.2 "I've had a cleaner before and it didn't last"**

That is the most common reason people come to us. A set day and a set team, with cover when somebody is away, and we tell you first rather than you finding out on the day.

## **11.3 "My house is too messy"**

That is the job. Tell us honestly how it is when you book so we allow the right time. Nothing you say will surprise us.

## **11.4 "It's probably expensive"**

You get the price before we start, on your actual place rather than a guess. And if you need less than you asked for, we will say so.

## **11.5 "I don't want to be locked into anything"**

No minimum term, no exit fee. Skip a visit when you are away and your spot stays yours.

## **11.6 "I should just do it myself"**

Plenty of people can. The question is whether it is getting done, and whether you want to keep it on the list.

## **11.7 "What if they break something"**

Say what the insurance position actually is. **\[CONFIRM with Teagan and state plainly.\]** A vague answer here loses the booking.

## **11.8 "What if I don't like the work"**

Tell us and we will come back and sort it. **\[CONFIRM the actual policy before this is published. Do not imply a guarantee that does not exist.\]**

# **12\. Proof and Evidence**

## **12.1 What We Use**

**Before and after images** from genuine TLB jobs, with client permission and no identifying detail. The single most persuasive asset available and the most underused.

**Real testimonials.** Two types carry the most weight: a long-standing client who names how long they have been with TLB, because tenure is the proof this business needs, and a client who has been with a few cleaners over the years, because the switcher story converts.

**Published task lists.** On deep cleaning particularly, publishing the room-by-room inclusions before somebody books does more than any adjective.

**Named towns.** Specificity is proof. A page naming Kyogle and Evans Head reads as local in a way "servicing the Northern Rivers" does not.

**Numbers, once confirmed.** A retention figure beats any adjective.

## **12.2 What We Never Do**

No fabricated testimonials, case studies or statistics.

No guarantee of an outcome, including bond-back and satisfaction guarantees.

No stock imagery presented as our own work.

No composite testimonials assembled from several clients.

# **13\. Booking and Systems**

## **13.1 Current**

Connecteam is used by the team for scheduling. It is workforce management, so it does not take bookings from the public or provide instant quoting on its own.

## **13.2 Direction**

Phase one is a quote calculator on the website that returns a price immediately and pushes the job into Connecteam through the Jobs API or Zapier.

**Instant pricing works for regular cleans**, where bedrooms, bathrooms and frequency genuinely predict the job. **It works badly for deep cleans, bond cleans and commercial**, where condition drives the price and a client's self-assessment is unreliable.

So: instant price on regular cleaning, fast quote on everything else. Copy across the site should reflect that split rather than promising instant pricing on jobs that cannot be priced sight-unseen.

# **14\. How TLB Should Appear in AI Answers**

People increasingly ask an AI assistant rather than a search engine. "Who are the best cleaners in Ballina", "how do I find a cleaner for an Airbnb in Byron", "what should a bond clean cost".

Those answers are assembled from content that is specific, structured and answers the question directly. Three things make TLB citable:

**Named towns, not regions.** An answer about Kyogle is built from content that mentions Kyogle.

**Published specifics.** Room-by-room task lists, what is included and excluded, how long a job takes. Vague marketing copy is not usable as an answer.

**Questions answered plainly.** FAQ sections written as real questions with direct answers, not as a marketing opportunity.

The practical instruction: every service page should contain at least one section that would work as a standalone answer to a question somebody would actually ask.

# **15\. Getting It Right**

## **15.1 The Opening Line**

**Wrong:** "Looking for a sparkling clean home? TLB Cleaning has you covered\!" *Superlative, exclamation mark, says nothing, could be any company in the country.*

**Right:** "TLB Cleaning is a Northern Rivers cleaning company. From Lismore, Alstonville and Ballina, up through Byron, Mullumbimby and Murwillumbah to Tweed Heads, and everywhere else the beautiful Northern Rivers encompasses." *Specific, local, names the whole region.*

## **15.2 The Team**

**Wrong:** "Our team of local mums work around school hours to bring care and attention to every home." *Describes the workforce rather than what a client gets. Also implies part-time availability.*

**Right:** "Everyone who works for TLB lives here. Our teams are spread right across the Northern Rivers, inland and on the coast, and they clean in the same towns they live in and shop in." *Same warmth, and it answers the question the reader actually has.*

## **15.3 Price**

**Wrong:** "We're not the cheapest, but you get what you pay for." *Defensive, and puts the word cheapest in the reader's head.*

**Right:** "You'll know the price before we start, not after. We quote on your actual place rather than a guess, and if it turns out you need less than you asked for, we'll say so." *Certainty, and the offer to talk them into the smaller job does more than any adjective.*

## **15.4 Competitors**

**Wrong:** "Unlike the big franchises, we actually turn up." *Defensive, invites the reader to wonder who we are arguing with.*

**Right:** Say nothing about them in the prose. Put it in a table row: "When someone is away — A national brand or app: a different contractor. An independent cleaner: rebook for another week. TLB: someone from your team covers, and we let you know first." *Same point, reads as information.*

## **15.5 A Deep Clean**

**Wrong:** "Let us transform your home with our premium deep cleaning service." *Transform implies the house is currently a problem. Premium signals price.*

**Right:** "Before the guests arrive, after the party, when you move in, or when it's just got away from you. A single clean that takes the house back to the start." *Names the actual moments people book one, and removes the embarrassment.*

## **15.6 Holiday Lets**

**Wrong:** "We offer professional cleaning services for Airbnb properties." *True and completely inert.*

**Right:** "We work to your booking calendar, not the other way around. No messaging a cleaner between guests, no wondering whether the turnover happened." *Names the thing that actually annoys them.*
