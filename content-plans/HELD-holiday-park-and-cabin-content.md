## 6. §7 "Worth knowing" — the accommodation version

**Not moved as-is: rewritten in place for restaurants.** The argument
transferred and the examples did not, so the hospitality page now carries a
restaurant version of this section. The accommodation original is kept here
because it is the better-known version and should be reinstated if this content

> **Count how many one-star reviews are about cleaning**
>
> Look at the one and two star reviews for any accommodation business in this
> region and count how many are about cleaning. It is most of them. Not the
> location, not the price, not the beds: hair in the shower, crumbs in the
> drawer, a bin that was not emptied, a bathroom that smelled. Each of those is
> a fifteen minute failure that sits on your listing for years and gets read by
> everyone deciding between you and the park up the road.
>
> The economics of that are brutal and they run the other way to how cleaning
> usually gets budgeted. Trimming a cabin turnover from ninety minutes to sixty
> saves you half an hour of labour and costs you a review, and the review costs
> more than the season's savings. Which is why the thing to buy here is not the
> lowest-priced turnover, it is a turnover that happens the same way every
> single time, by people who have done that cabin before and know which drawer
> the last guest used.

## 9. Non-branded FAQ — "How long should a cabin turnover take?"

> **How long should a cabin turnover take?**
>
> A one bedroom cabin with linen usually runs to about an hour with two
> cleaners, and a two or three bedroom cottage closer to ninety minutes to two
> hours. The variable that matters is not the size, it is whether the previous
> guest cooked. A kitchenette that has been used properly adds twenty minutes on
> its own, which is why a flat per-cabin rate with no scope behind it tends to
> come apart in peak.

---

## 10. Non-branded FAQ — "How often should holiday park amenities be cleaned?"

> **How often should holiday park amenities be cleaned?**
>
> Twice a day at minimum in peak and generally three times through the busiest
> weeks, with a full clean in the morning, a mid-afternoon check and restock,
> and an evening pass. Off peak, daily is usually enough. Amenities blocks are
> the most reviewed part of any park because they are the one thing guests use
> that they did not choose, so under-servicing them shows up in ratings faster
> than anything else.

Verification note carried over from the page: holiday parks in NSW are
regulated as caravan parks and manufactured home estates, with amenities
standards, and are inspected by local councils. Correct in substance and kept
general, with no clause number quoted.
